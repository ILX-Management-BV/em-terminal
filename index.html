Macro Brief — The Signal Alignment

When oil, bond yields, and credit spreads move in the same direction simultaneously, it typically signals a regime shift. That alignment is now in place. The Iran conflict has pushed energy back to the centre of the macro conversation. When oil moves 40%+ in under two weeks, financial markets do not adjust linearly — they re-price the entire distribution of outcomes.

1. Oil is now the primary macro variable.

Brent surged from ~$68 to $119.50 in ten sessions before partially retracing to $99. The Hormuz closure has removed approximately 20 million barrels per day of global transit capacity. This is not a risk premium — it is a physical supply shock. The Brent futures curve is in steep backwardation ($99 spot vs ~$86 for Sep), which tells you the market is pricing a short-duration disruption. If that assumption is wrong, the entire curve reprices higher.

2. Bond markets are repricing the inflation path, not growth risk.

US 10-year yields are at 4.257% in a week when the economy shed jobs and global risk-off would normally drive safe-haven buying. Breakeven inflation has jumped. The market is telling you that even with weakening economic data, the Fed cannot cut into an energy-driven inflation impulse. Rate cut expectations have collapsed. This creates the textbook stagflation combination: tighter financial conditions meeting weaker growth.

3. EM credit is in the early stages of systematic repricing.

EMBI+ spreads widened ~50bps in a week. Egypt CDS at 371bps (+27% 1-month). Turkey CDS at 250bps, war-proximity embedded. When credit, rates, and commodities align directionally, the macro environment has shifted. 7 of 10 scenario signals are now in Adverse or Tail territory. What matters now is duration — and that is determined by geopolitics, not economics.

None of these signals individually point to a crisis. But their simultaneous alignment points to a regime shift. The portfolio's 26.1% war-core cluster (TRY + EGP + JOD) and 13.1% corridor cluster (UZ + GE + AM) are the primary transmission nodes. The 12.8% oil-exporter hedge cluster is the partial offset. Duration is the swing variable.
Transmission Channels — How the Shock Propagates to the Portfolio
1. Energy → Inflation

Oil at $99 feeds through to headline CPI within 4–6 weeks via gasoline, jet fuel, petrochemicals, fertiliser, and food transport. Turkey imports ~93% of oil — energy import bill up ~$20bn annualised. Egypt gas import costs doubling. This channel is mechanical and unavoidable for oil-importing portfolio countries.

2. Inflation → CB Paralysis

Turkey CBRT faces stagflation: rising imported inflation vs slowing domestic growth. Egypt CBE constrained by IMF programme. Pakistan SBP trapped by balance of payments. The classic EM trilemma (defend FX, fight inflation, support growth — pick one) is acute for the three highest-risk portfolio clusters.

3. Tighter Conditions → EM Capital Flows

US higher-for-longer + stronger DXY (100+) compresses EM dollar liquidity. EM central banks that defend their currencies burn reserves. Those that don't see FX depreciation (TRY key: 14.5% wt, EGP key: 8.7% wt). The feedback loop: wider spreads → more capital flight → weaker FX → more inflation → wider spreads.

4. FX Depreciation → Portfolio FX Impact

TRY at 44.19 — Adverse entry (47–50 range). EGP 1-month change +11.9% — already Tail signal. These are the two largest positions (14.5% + 8.7% = 23.2% combined). A 10% further TRY depreciation alone costs ~1.45% of portfolio value. Corridor cluster (UZ, GE, AM) faces secondary pressure via disrupted remittance and trade flows.

5. Oil Exporters → Natural Hedge

Ecuador (7.2%), Nigeria (1.4%), Angola (1.4%), Azerbaijan (1.4%), Colombia (1.4%) = 12.8% of portfolio. At $99+ oil, fiscal positions improve, Eurobond credit compresses, spread tightening partially offsets war-core losses. Ecuador dollarised — no FX risk, pure credit play. Angola and Nigeria benefit on a ~$1bn additional revenue per $10 oil move.

6. Global Risk-Off → EM-Wide Contagion

Even insulated positions (MX, CL, BR, VN, SG) face second-order pressure from DXY strength and risk-appetite contraction. UST 10Y higher-for-longer raises the discount rate on all EM credit. EMBI+ 485bps compresses valuation on all 69 positions. Singapore (AAA) and Lithuania (A+) anchor the portfolio; LatAm exposure largely rides out global risk-off without war-specific transmission.

Scenario Framework — Iran War Regional Impact on Portfolio (13 Mar 2026)

BASE CASE 50%

Ceasefire or de-escalation within 2–3 weeks. Hormuz reopens conditionally. Oil stabilises $80–95. US–Iran back-channel via Oman/Qatar advances.

  • Türkiye (14.5%) — TRY partially recovers to 42–43; CBRT holds; energy import shock fades. CDS retreats to ~210.
  • Egypt (8.7%) — EGP stabilises ~50–52; Suez revenue partially recovers; IMF tranche on track. CDS to ~300.
  • Jordan (2.9%) — USD peg intact; Gulf financing continues; regional spill-over contained.
  • Uzbekistan (8.7%) — Russia–Central Asia corridor resumes; remittance inflows recover.
  • Oil importers (BD, VN, RO, PK, LT, TH) — CAD pressure eases; FX broadly stable.
  • Oil exporters (EC, NG, CO, AO, AZ) — War premium fades; modest price decline vs today but still supportive.
  • LatAm/isolated (MX, AR, CL, BR) — Unaffected; macro trajectory governs.
  • Est. portfolio weighted FX impact: −3% to −5% vs Dec-25 (losses mostly already embedded).

ADVERSE 35%

War extends to Q2 2026; Hormuz intermittently blocked. Oil embeds at $100–120. Stagflation risk intensifies; sustained EM oil-importer pressure.

  • Türkiye (14.5%) — TRY to 47–50; energy import bill +$8bn/yr; CBRT on hold. CDS widens to 320–380. Current account further deteriorates.
  • Egypt (8.7%) — EGP to 57–62; Suez revenue −$1bn/mo sustained; IMF emergency tranche required. CDS to 500–700.
  • Jordan (2.9%) — Peg maintained via Gulf emergency support; fiscal deterioration; Eurobond refinancing risk elevated.
  • Uzbekistan (8.7%) — Remittance corridor disruption; Russia re-export dynamics strained; UZS managed depreciation.
  • Pakistan (1.4%) — Iran border closure; energy import cost shock; PKR −5–8%; IMF programme tested.
  • Bangladesh (2.9%) — Garment export margins squeezed by energy costs; BDT −3–5%; CAD widens.
  • Oil exporters hedge (EC 7.2%, NG 1.4%, CO 1.4%, AO 1.4%, AZ 1.4%) — Partially offset losses; $110+ oil supportive for fiscal positions.
  • Armenia / Georgia (2.9% each) — Transit corridor disruption; Armenia energy import exposure.
  • Est. portfolio weighted FX impact: −8% to −12% vs Dec-25 (war-core + importers drag; oil exporters partial offset).

TAIL RISK 15%

Full escalation: Iran strikes Saudi/UAE infrastructure; Hormuz fully blocked for 3+ months. 1973 embargo analogy. Global recession scenario.

  • Türkiye (14.5%) — CBRT emergency hike to 45%+; TRY to 52–58; sovereign CDS 600+; potential capital flow restrictions.
  • Egypt (8.7%) — EGP to 65–75; capital controls possible; IMF SDR emergency draw; repo market stress. CDS 1,000+.
  • Jordan (2.9%) — USD peg fragile; Gulf aid uncertain if wider conflict; Eurobond default risk rises.
  • Pakistan (1.4%) — Iran border fully closed; external financing freeze; CDS 1,200+; emergency IMF activation.
  • Bangladesh / Vietnam (2.9% each) — Shipping disruption + energy shock; BDT/VND −10–15%; supply chain breakdown.
  • Uzbekistan / Armenia (8.7% / 1.4%) — Full corridor disruption; remittances collapse; FX reserves burn.
  • Oil exporter hedge (EC, NG, CO, AO, AZ = ~12.8%) — Revenue windfall ($150–200 oil) but market access disruption; partial shield only.
  • Singapore / Lithuania / Chile / Uruguay — Flight-to-quality anchor positions; relatively insulated.
  • Est. portfolio weighted FX impact: −20% to −30% vs Dec-25. War-core cluster losses deep; oil exporters insufficient to offset.

Five Variables That Determine the Scenario
1

Strait of Hormuz Tanker Traffic

The single most important variable. If tanker traffic meaningfully resumes within 2 weeks, the Base Case holds: oil drops $15–20, TRY and EGP recover, war-core cluster (26.1%) stabilises. If the Strait remains physically contested beyond end-March, the Adverse scenario becomes the central case: EGP to 57–62, TRY to 47–50, portfolio FX impact −8–12%. Monitor daily vessel tracking (Kpler, MarineTraffic), insurance rates for Gulf transit, and IRGC naval posture statements. The Brent futures backwardation curve (spot $99 vs Sep $86) is pricing a short disruption — if Sep begins rising toward spot, the market is abandoning the quick-resolution thesis.

2

Iran Leadership Signals — Negotiate or Escalate?

New Iranian Supreme Leader appointed by the Assembly of Experts. The critical binary: does the new leadership signal willingness to negotiate (de-escalation path, Oman/Qatar back-channel) or doubles down (Hormuz fully closed, Gulf state strikes continue)? Watch for: indirect diplomatic channel reopening, any pause in Iranian strikes on Gulf civilian infrastructure, and back-channel signals via Qatar. A confirmed ceasefire framework would be the single largest positive catalyst for the portfolio's war-core cluster.

3

Turkey CBRT & Egypt CBE Policy Response

Combined weight 23.2% of portfolio. Turkey CBRT currently at 37% — if energy pass-through forces an emergency hike (precedent: +200bps in earlier 2026 episode), TRY could stabilise 43–45 and limit portfolio FX loss on the largest single position. Egypt CBE faces an IMF programme constraint — any EGP devaluation beyond 57 requires IMF emergency tranche consultation. Watch Monthly: CBRT meeting dates, CBE reserve adequacy data, Egypt external debt service schedule for Q2 2026. These are the two most actionable policy signals for portfolio management.

4

Gulf Sovereign Wealth Fund Flows

ADIA (UAE ~$700bn+), QIA (Qatar ~$500bn), KIA (Kuwait ~$900bn), and PIF (Saudi) collectively manage $3+ trillion. If these funds begin liquidating global portfolio positions to fund domestic fiscal needs — defence spending, reconstruction, economic stabilisation — the sell pressure transmits the Gulf shock into global asset markets. This is the channel most investors overlook. Monitor: Gulf sovereign bond auction results, any reduction in Gulf FDI commitments, and unusual selling patterns in European and US equities. This is the mechanism by which a regional energy war becomes a global financial shock.

5

EM FX Reserve Drawdowns & Peg Sustainability

The portfolio's three most vulnerable FX positions: TRY (managed float, reserves thin), EGP (managed, already −12% 1-month), JOD (USD peg, Gulf-aid dependent). A JOD peg break (2.9% weight) would be a binary event — immediate 15–35% devaluation. EGP capital controls (8.7% weight) would freeze mark-to-market pricing. Watch: Weekly Egyptian FX reserve data from CBE, Jordan's external financing confirmations from Gulf states, and Pakistan's SBP reserve adequacy (1.4% weight, Iran border stress). If India's RBI begins burning reserves aggressively to defend the rupee, it signals the shock has moved from frontier to core EM.
