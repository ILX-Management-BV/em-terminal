Macro Brief — The Signal Alignment (AI-generated scenario analysis)

When oil, bond yields, and credit spreads move in the same direction simultaneously, it typically signals a regime shift. That alignment is now in place. The Iran conflict has pushed energy back to the centre of the macro conversation. When oil moves 40%+ in under two weeks, financial markets do not adjust linearly — they re-price the entire distribution of outcomes.

1. Oil is now the primary macro variable.

Brent surged from ~$68 to $119.50 in ten sessions and is still holding above $110 as of 4 May. The Hormuz disruption has kept a material share of global transit capacity at risk. This is not just a risk premium — it is a physical supply shock. The futures curve still points to markets pricing a finite disruption; if that assumption proves wrong, the entire curve reprices higher.

2. Bond markets are repricing the inflation path, not growth risk.

US 10-year yields are holding near 4.41% even as ceasefire hopes periodically improve risk sentiment. Breakeven inflation remains elevated. The market is still telling you that even with softer growth data, the Fed cannot ease cleanly into an energy-driven inflation impulse. That leaves the textbook stagflation mix in place: tighter financial conditions meeting weaker growth.

3. EM credit is in the early stages of systematic repricing.

EMBI+ proxy levels are holding near ~267bps, while war-core sovereign stress remains above base-case comfort levels: Egypt CDS is ~339bps and Turkey CDS is ~244bps. Relief in global beta has not fully normalised the countries closest to the conflict. What matters now is duration — and that is determined by geopolitics, not economics.

None of these signals individually point to a crisis. But their simultaneous alignment points to a regime shift. The portfolio's 26.1% war-core cluster (TRY + EGP + JOD) and 13.1% corridor cluster (UZ + GE + AM) are the primary transmission nodes. The 12.8% oil-exporter hedge cluster is the partial offset. Duration is the swing variable.
Transmission Channels — How the Shock Propagates to the Portfolio
1. Energy → Inflation

Oil still above $100 feeds through to headline CPI within 4–6 weeks via gasoline, jet fuel, petrochemicals, fertiliser, and food transport. Turkey imports ~93% of oil; Egypt remains exposed through gas and fuel costs. This channel is mechanical and unavoidable for oil-importing portfolio countries.

2. Inflation → CB Paralysis

Turkey CBRT faces stagflation: rising imported inflation vs slowing domestic growth. Egypt CBE constrained by IMF programme. Pakistan SBP trapped by balance of payments. The classic EM trilemma (defend FX, fight inflation, support growth — pick one) is acute for the three highest-risk portfolio clusters.

3. Tighter Conditions → EM Capital Flows

Even with DXY easing toward 98.3, the broader higher-for-longer backdrop still compresses EM dollar liquidity. EM central banks that defend their currencies burn reserves. Those that do not see FX depreciation in the war-core cluster. The feedback loop remains the same: wider spreads → capital flight → weaker FX → more inflation → wider spreads.

4. FX Depreciation → Portfolio FX Impact

TRY is at 45.20 and EGP at 53.45. That is off the late-March extremes, but still above base-case comfort levels for the two largest positions (14.5% + 8.7% = 23.2% combined). A 10% further TRY depreciation alone still costs ~1.45% of portfolio value. Corridor cluster (UZ, GE, AM) faces secondary pressure via disrupted remittance and trade flows.

5. Oil Exporters → Natural Hedge

Ecuador (7.2%), Nigeria (1.4%), Angola (1.4%), Azerbaijan (1.4%), Colombia (1.4%) = 12.8% of portfolio. With oil still above $100, fiscal positions improve and spread tightening partially offsets war-core losses. Ecuador is dollarised, so this remains a pure credit play; Angola and Nigeria still benefit materially from higher oil revenues.

6. Global Risk-Off → EM-Wide Contagion

Even insulated positions (MX, CL, BR, VN, SG) still face second-order pressure from restrictive U.S. rates and fragile risk appetite. The EMBI+ proxy is holding near 267bps rather than the late-March spike, but war-core stress remains elevated. Singapore and Lithuania remain among the portfolio's higher-quality sovereign anchors; LatAm exposure still rides out global risk-off better than war-adjacent holdings.

Five Variables That Determine the Scenario
1

Strait of Hormuz Tanker Traffic

The single most important variable. If tanker traffic meaningfully resumes within 2 weeks, the Base Case holds: oil drops $15–20, TRY and EGP recover, war-core cluster (26.1%) stabilises. If the Strait remains physically contested over the next few weeks, the Adverse scenario becomes the central case: EGP to 57–62, TRY to 47–50, portfolio FX impact −8–12%. Monitor daily vessel tracking (Kpler, MarineTraffic), insurance rates for Gulf transit, and IRGC naval posture statements. Brent still above $110 is pricing a finite disruption — if deferred contracts begin rising toward spot, the market is abandoning the quick-resolution thesis.

2

Iran Leadership Signals — Negotiate or Escalate?

New Iranian Supreme Leader appointed by the Assembly of Experts. The critical binary: does the new leadership signal willingness to negotiate (de-escalation path, Oman/Qatar back-channel) or doubles down (Hormuz fully closed, Gulf state strikes continue)? Watch for: indirect diplomatic channel reopening, any pause in Iranian strikes on Gulf civilian infrastructure, and back-channel signals via Qatar. A confirmed ceasefire framework would be the single largest positive catalyst for the portfolio's war-core cluster.

3

Turkey CBRT & Egypt CBE Policy Response

Combined weight 23.2% of portfolio. Turkey CBRT currently at 37% — if energy pass-through forces an emergency hike (precedent: +200bps in earlier 2026 episode), TRY could stabilise 43–45 and limit portfolio FX loss on the largest single position. Egypt CBE faces an IMF programme constraint — any EGP devaluation beyond 57 requires IMF emergency tranche consultation. Watch Monthly: CBRT meeting dates, CBE reserve adequacy data, Egypt external debt service schedule for Q2 2026. These are the two most actionable policy signals for portfolio management.

4

Gulf Sovereign Wealth Fund Flows

ADIA (UAE ~$700bn+), QIA (Qatar ~$500bn), KIA (Kuwait ~$900bn), and PIF (Saudi) collectively manage $3+ trillion. If these funds begin liquidating global portfolio positions to fund domestic fiscal needs — defence spending, reconstruction, economic stabilisation — the sell pressure transmits the Gulf shock into global asset markets. This is the channel most investors overlook. Monitor: Gulf sovereign bond auction results, any reduction in Gulf FDI commitments, and unusual selling patterns in European and US equities. This is the mechanism by which a regional energy war becomes a global financial shock.

5

EM FX Reserve Drawdowns & Peg Sustainability

The portfolio's three most vulnerable FX positions: TRY (managed float, reserves thin), EGP (managed, still above base-case comfort levels), JOD (USD peg, Gulf-aid dependent). A JOD peg break (2.9% weight) would be a binary event — immediate 15–35% devaluation. EGP capital controls (8.7% weight) would freeze mark-to-market pricing. Watch: Weekly Egyptian FX reserve data from CBE, Jordan's external financing confirmations from Gulf states, and Pakistan's SBP reserve adequacy (1.4% weight, Iran border stress). If India's RBI begins burning reserves aggressively to defend the rupee, it signals the shock has moved from frontier to core EM.
